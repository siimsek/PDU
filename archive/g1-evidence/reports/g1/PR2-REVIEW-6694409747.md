## Decision

**A guaranteed, implementable OVP chain is not established.** The TLV1701-Q1 remains a wide-input monitor candidate, not a timing-qualified sole OVP: its 460 ns value is typical and the maximum is not specified. The faster LTC6752 candidate's 5.7 ns maximum is specified at 50 mV overdrive under the datasheet's VCM=300 mV test setup; the candidate senses near 2.5 V at trip. Also, the present 17.95 kΩ/5.00 kΩ divider drives its input above the specified common-mode range during the 40.709 V bus fault. Its input protection and output translation would need redesign and further verification. Both candidates remain **OPEN** for actual O1 ≤12.0 V acceptance.

The 0.5 µs number is retained as a timing screen; it is not substituted for the binding O1 peak limit. The numerical screens below use explicit assumptions and do not turn either criterion into a silicon or physical PASS.

The 191 ns MOSFET term is only an RC proxy from maximum gate charge and the LTC7001 4 Ω maximum pull-down resistance. It is not a characterized two-FET turn-off time. The LTC7001's 70 ns maximum input-to-output delay is specified for a 1 nF load; the two selected FETs have substantially larger input/gate charge, so this number is not transferred as a guaranteed loaded delay. The MOSFET Qg specification is itself stated at VDS=50 V, ID=100 A and VGS=10 V, not the fault operating point.

For the stated scalar corners, the closed-form threshold extrema were checked by enumerating all 16 VREF/offset/Rtop/Rbottom endpoint combinations; the extrema matched with 0 V difference. The differential-slew and capacitor-charge routes to comparator overdrive also matched with 0 s difference. These are independent algorithms/formulations over the same assumptions, not independent silicon or physical validation.

### LTC7001 bootstrap correction

The LTC7001 datasheet requires `CB > external MOSFET QG / 1 V`; if external CG is used it requires `CB > MOSFET QG / 1 V + 10 × CG`. Two CSD19536KTT FETs have 153 nC maximum Qg each at the datasheet test condition, so this candidate needs **more than 306 nF effective BST-to-TS capacitance**, before any CG contribution. The prior 0.1 µF candidate does not satisfy that inequality. No capacitor MPN is selected; DC-bias/tolerance loss must be included when one is selected.

### Comparator rail loss and threshold caveats

- **TLV1701-Q1:** its output is open collector and can be pulled to another rail while the comparator is powered. The datasheet does not guarantee the fault-output state with V+ absent; do not treat an unpowered comparator as an asserted fault. The two LDO PG pins clear the latch while their input conditions are met, but the TPS7A16-Q1 PG behavior is specified only with VIN ≥3 V.
- **LTC6752-2:** the output is CMOS on separate VDD. At 1.8 V it cannot directly guarantee the required 5 V CLR_N interface; it needs a low-dominant level interface. No valid comparator output state is specified for both supply rails absent. The current divider also needs a clamp or redesign to keep the input below VCC+0.1 V during the high-voltage fault. The published propagation-delay maximum uses VCM=300 mV; candidate threshold VCM is about 2.5 V, so the max is not assumed to cover this circuit.
- Threshold ranges are **partial HESAP**, not final trip limits: LTC6752 input-bias effect at the actual common-mode point, resistor TCR, leakage, hysteresis and comparator-to-divider loading were not closed.

The same datasheet lists hFE minima at IC=0.1 mA and 1 mA, but not at the calculated Q_INP_RELEASE collector current of about 0.532 mA. The forced-beta ratios above are design screens, not a substitute for a guaranteed gain/saturation point at the candidate load.

## TPS259824O input-loss and OUT−IN evidence

TI's TPS25982 Rev D datasheet, §6.1 p.6, lists `VIN ≤30 V` and `VOUT ≤ min(30 V, VIN+0.3 V)` as absolute-maximum pin limits. §6.3 p.7 recommends VIN 2.7–24 V and VOUT≤VIN+0.3 V. These are pin operating/stress boundaries; they do not promise internal reverse blocking, safe reverse current, or an OUT discharge action when IN disappears. No explicit reverse-current or input-loss behavior guarantee was found in the official datasheet.

TI lists an official TPS259824O PSpice model (`SLVMD37`, encrypted). Its readable header identifies a PSpice 16.2 model and selected start-up, current-limit, OV/UV, dV/dt, PG/Fault and thermal features. It does not establish silicon guarantees for reverse current or power-off OUT−IN behavior. PSpice/Cadence was unavailable in this environment, so the encrypted model was not run and was not converted to an ngspice PASS.

The retained R6 fixture measurements are [`ovp-control-chain-followup.json`](ovp-control-chain-followup.json), [`r6-efuse-out-in.log`](sim/r6-efuse-out-in.log), and [`r6-dual-rail-gate-loss.log`](sim/r6-dual-rail-gate-loss.log): forced-open behavioral eFuse paths showed O1 OUT−IN≈10.9971 V for ≥11.099 ms and S2E OUT−IN≈9.36228 V for 3.921 ms. In the `CO_OPEN` case both ideal common-switch and eFuse paths were forced open at 900 µs, before the RAW fault began at 1 ms. This is a precharged/input-loss negative fixture only; it is not actual TPS259824O silicon behavior or a real detection-and-turn-off sequence.
